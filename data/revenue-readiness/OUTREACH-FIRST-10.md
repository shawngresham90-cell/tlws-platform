## Discovery-call checklist

Ten minutes, and the goal is to *disqualify fast* — a business that should not
buy is a better outcome than one that buys and regrets it.

- [ ] Confirm who you are speaking to and whether they can decide.
- [ ] Confirm the listing is theirs and the details are right. Take corrections.
- [ ] Ask what a new customer is worth to them. Do not tell them; let them say it.
- [ ] Ask whether they advertise anywhere now, and whether it worked.
- [ ] Explain what Sponsored means before they ask.
- [ ] State the price plainly. Once. Do not repeat it nervously.
- [ ] Say the honest thing about measurement before they ask for numbers.
- [ ] Ask if they want it, or want to wait for figures. Accept "wait".
- [ ] If yes: confirm monthly or annual, and the start date.
- [ ] Agree the next step and a date. Write it in `next_action`.

Disqualify — politely, on the call — if: they want a guarantee you cannot give;
they want their competitor removed; they want to edit the listing themselves;
they are a franchise with no local marketing budget; or the price is clearly a
stretch. Leave the free listing in place and thank them.

> • It will be labelled Sponsored on the page.

>   annual gets the unused whole months back.
> • I have not promised you traffic, leads or results, and I am not going to.
>
> If any of that is wrong, tell me before I invoice.

Every line of that is a fact or a commitment about money. Nothing in it is a
prediction about their business.

## Rules for all ten

- Nothing may be sent without separate approval.
- Claim first. The price is the second conversation, always.
- Never quote traffic, leads, ranking or results. There are none to quote.
- Never say a listing is verified, claimed or featured when it is not.
- One follow-up. Then stop.
- Anyone who asks to be left alone is marked and never contacted again.
- Anyone who asks for their listing to come down gets it taken down, with no
  negotiation and no counter-offer.
